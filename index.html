What the agents do, and what stays yours

They split the job
A lead agent plans the work, starts as many workers as it needs, and changes the plan when a result or a message from you calls for it.
They use your real apps
Each browser agent gets its own Chrome window. Others work in Mac apps such as Word, Slack and Cursor, in the background, with windows arranged so nothing overlaps.
They ask before acting as you
Sign-ins, anything sent in your name, and steps only you can do arrive as a card on the window that needs you. Nothing is sent until you click.
They wait while you type
An agent holds its next action until you pause. You can also pause any agent, take over its window, and hand it back.
They get better at your work
After each job the lead writes down what it learned as a skill, a plain file you can read, edit or delete. The next job starts from it.

One agent, two desks

In a team, an agent can read something on your Mac and write it on a teammate's. While it rests, its cursor sits on both screens. When one of you calls it, it goes to that screen and leaves the other.

  • A teammate's session cannot use your Mac until you allow it, each time.
  • Everyone can talk to the lead, and every message carries a name.
  • What the team learns is remembered with the person it came from.

Install it

  1. Download the app and drag it to Applications. Apple silicon or Intel.
  2. Open it the first time from System Settings. This early build is not yet notarized by Apple, so macOS will stop it once. Go to Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway.
  3. Add your Anthropic API key. The app asks for it the first time you run a job. Agents run on your own key, which stays on your Mac.
  4. Press Command Shift Space and say what you want done. The same keys hide the bar and bring it back.

Before you install

This is an early version. Agents act inside your own accounts, and they will make mistakes. Start with jobs you can check, keep an eye on the cards, and expect each job to cost API credit.

Browser agents need Google Chrome. Controlling other Mac apps needs one extra component, which the app explains when you first ask for it.
